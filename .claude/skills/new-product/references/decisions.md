# Decide these before writing code

Each one has already cost money by being answered late, or by being answered silently by a
default nobody chose. Get a real answer from the owner. "We'll see" is not an answer.

## Money

**Which currency does the tax authority need?** For a Ukrainian Individual Entrepreneur with
monobank acquiring, the answer is hryvnia, code 980, and there is no negotiating with it.

**What does the price tag say?** A different question. If it says euro while the charge is
hryvnia, the price is a fixed peg, it must be re-pegged as the rate drifts, and the checkout
must disclose the charge currency. Both figures then need a test tying them together,
because no screen shows the drift.

**What is free, and what is the wall?** The free allowance is the marketing. Decide the
number before building the counter, not after.

**One-off, subscription, or balance?** A prepaid balance is a voucher: decide what happens
to unspent balance on refund and on account deletion before selling any.

## Audience

**Which country are the customers in?** This decides the currency conversation, the sales
tax registration trigger, the consent banner, and which language matters.

**Which languages, and who translates?** Every locale multiplies the cost of every piece of
copy forever. Five was a lot for one person. Start with one and add on evidence.

**What does a customer see on their bank statement?** If it will not match the price tag,
say so at checkout.

## Product

**What is the one thing it does?** Write the sentence before the first file.

**What does a stranger see before signing up?** If the answer is "a login box", the product
has no search traffic and cannot be reviewed by an advertising network. Public content is
not a growth task added later; it is a launch requirement.

**What is the name, and is the domain free?** Check both together.

## Scope traps

**Does this need accounts at all?** Accounts bring password reset, verification, deletion,
export, and a data-protection surface. If the product works without them, that is months
saved.

**Does this need email at all?** Email brings deliverability, bounce handling, sending
limits and a bulk-sender migration. Every scheduled message is a thing that can fail
silently at three in the morning.

**Does this need payments on day one?** A product with no paying customers does not need a
reconciliation sweep, a dunning state machine or a tax receipt integration.

Answering "no" to any of these three is the largest available saving on the whole project.
